I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Section on Neuroscience and Novel Therapeutics (NNT) at the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH). 

I received my B.A. in cognitive science (neuroscience concentration) and psychology from Northwestern University. After graduation, I spent three years as a research assistant at the Section on Integrative Neuroimaging at NIMH. I recently completed my Ph.D. (and non-terminal M.S.) in clinical psychology at the University of Maryland, College Park after completing my clinical internship at the Ohio State University Wexner Medical Center.

# Research

Anxiety disorders, depression, and other internalizing disorders impose a staggering burden on public health and often emerge or recur during times of stress. Existing treatments are far from curative for many, underscoring the need to clarify the factors governing the development and maintenance of these often-debilitating illnesses. My research aims to look across the lifespan to understand (1) the neurobiological substrates of neuroticism/negative emotionality (N/NE) and other risk-conferring emotional traits, and the relevance of threat- and safety-related brain circuits to (2) real-world mood and (3) future internalizing symptoms and disorders. My work integrates multiple techniques, including fMRI, clinical/life-stress assessments, smartphone ecological momentary assessment (EMA), and advanced statistical approaches in at-risk and clinical populations. This work has the potential to inform mechanistic work and provide a quantitative rationale for prioritizing specific neurobiological and psychosocial targets for therapeutics development and repurposing. To date, my research has yielded 16 publications (4 first-author), 56 posters (21 first-author), and 6 first-author oral presentations. I have been fortunate to receive several honors, including a NIMH F31-NRSA fellowship, two NSF-GRFP Honorable Mentions, and the University of Maryland Graduate School's Michael J. Pelczar Award for Excellence in Graduate Study.
